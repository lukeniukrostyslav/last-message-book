## Short blurb
Alexei Morozov receives a message telling him he has thirty days left and warning him not to trust the person who comes first. The next message contains a photograph apparently taken tomorrow — with Alexei standing beside journalist Elena Varga.

What begins as a suspicious technical anomaly becomes an investigation into a secret predictive-risk project Alexei left six years ago. The deeper he goes, the more the evidence turns against the version of his own past he has been telling himself.

The system behind the messages does not see the future. It knows how people behave, what schedules exist, what evidence can be staged, and when information can be released. But its real purpose is more personal: to force Alexei to choose whether truth is worth exposing when the evidence also exposes him.

## Full synopsis — spoiler-inclusive
Alexei Morozov, an independent digital-forensics specialist and former participant in a confidential predictive-risk project, receives an anonymous warning: he has thirty days and should not trust the person who comes first. A second message contains a photograph apparently timestamped for the following day, showing Alexei beside investigative journalist Elena Varga.

Alexei initially assumes fraud. With former colleague Daniel Weiss, he examines the image, metadata and transmission path. The investigation leads back to the predictive-risk project Alexei left six years earlier. Elena is independently investigating the same project and knows details Alexei has avoided confronting.

The messages escalate. A second photograph appears to predict a conversation. Alexei eventually realizes that the messages are not supernatural predictions: they are outputs of an automated protocol called M, assembled from archived project data, schedules, behavioural models, staged evidence and human-authored instructions. The famous future-looking photograph was manufactured in advance and released at a calculated moment.

The investigation also forces Alexei to confront his own history. Archived credentials and missing modelling layers show that he was not merely an outside observer. He helped build the behavioural prediction layer and later tried to distance himself from what the project became. A buried incident demonstrates that predictions produced by the project influenced a real-world decision with serious human consequences.

As Day 30 approaches, former insiders attempt to destroy the archive. Alexei learns that preserving the evidence is not enough: the surviving record must be connected to an accountable human witness. M's final instruction points him toward a redundant archive while making clear that following it will expose his own role.

Alexei chooses disclosure over self-protection. He records a complete statement accepting his responsibility alongside the project's abuses. Daniel and Elena distribute independently verifiable copies. When the scheduled deletion occurs, it destroys the original controlled environment but cannot erase the surviving evidence.

The investigation becomes public. Elena publishes the story; Sofia and Alexei begin repairing their relationship; Alexei faces professional and legal consequences rather than receiving absolution for exposing the truth.

Book 1 resolves the central mystery: M is an automated behavioural communication protocol, the future photograph was staged, Day 30 was the archive-deletion window, and Alexei's accountability is part of the mechanism's final condition.

Afterward, Alexei receives an unexplained 08:16 message. It is not identified as coming from M. He chooses not to treat it as an instruction. The archive remains, the truth is public, and for the first time Alexei decides his next step without being directed.

## Pitch / positioning copy
**Core hook:** A forensic investigator receives a photograph from tomorrow and has thirty days to discover why someone knows what he will do — before the archive containing the truth is deleted.

**Genre:** Psychological thriller / mystery / technology / human drama.

**Central promise:** A closed, evidence-driven mystery in which the apparent prediction of the future is ultimately explained through behavioural modelling, staged evidence and controlled information release, while the protagonist discovers that the investigation implicates him.

**Book 1 promise:** The central mechanism and archive mystery are resolved. The final unexplained message functions only as a controlled sequel thread.
